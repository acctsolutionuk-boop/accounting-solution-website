Worked example, standard scheme. In a quarter a company invoices £60,000 plus £12,000 VAT, and buys £30,000 of goods and services with £6,000 VAT. Output VAT is £12,000, input VAT is £6,000, so it pays HMRC £6,000. Returns and payment are due one month and seven days after the end of the quarter.

The Flat Rate Scheme can save time but rarely saves money for businesses with significant costs, and a limited cost trader must use 16.5%. Which scheme suits you depends on your margins and how much VAT you reclaim, so we run the numbers before you choose.

Making Tax Digital for VAT

Every VAT-registered business must keep digital records and file through compatible software. A late return earns a penalty point, and a financial penalty follows once the points threshold is reached, so filing on time matters even when there is no VAT to pay.

Frequently Asked Questions

When do I need to register for VAT?

You must register when your taxable turnover exceeds £90,000 in any rolling 12-month period. You can also register voluntarily below this threshold, which is beneficial if you sell mainly to VAT-registered businesses.

What is Making Tax Digital for VAT?

MTD for VAT requires VAT-registered businesses to keep digital records and submit returns via approved software. It has been mandatory for all VAT-registered businesses since April 2022.

How much is the VAT registration threshold?

£90,000 of taxable turnover in any rolling 12 months. You must register within 30 days of the end of the month you exceed it, or if you expect to exceed it in a single 30-day period.

Which VAT scheme should I use?

It depends on your costs and margins. Standard accounting suits businesses with lots of VAT to reclaim, while the Flat Rate Scheme suits some low-cost service businesses. We compare the schemes on your actual figures before you choose.
