What good bookkeeping looks like

  • Every bank transaction matched to an invoice, bill or category, and the bank reconciled to the statement each month
  • Sales and purchase invoices recorded on the right dates, with VAT coded correctly if you are registered
  • Receipts and supporting documents attached to transactions, not left in a shoebox
  • Director's loan, payroll and VAT control accounts that agree to the underlying records
  • A month-end review that catches errors while they are easy to fix

Making Tax Digital for VAT already requires digital records, and Making Tax Digital for Income Tax will require the same for sole traders and landlords over the income thresholds. Good digital books are the foundation for both.

Example. A business shows a £1,200 payment from a customer on its bank feed. Recorded as "sales" without an invoice, the VAT and the customer's balance are both wrong. Matched to the £1,000 plus £200 VAT invoice, the ledger, the VAT return and the debtor list all agree.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is bookkeeping and why does my business need it?

Bookkeeping is the systematic recording of all business income and expenditure. It is the foundation for tax returns, understanding profit, and managing cash flow. HMRC requires businesses to keep adequate records.

Do you offer Xero and QuickBooks bookkeeping in Brighton?

Yes. We are experienced with both platforms and can set up, migrate, or manage your books on either. We also provide training so you can handle day-to-day entries yourself if preferred.

How long do I need to keep business records?

Self-employed people keep them for five years after the 31 January submission deadline. Limited companies and VAT records are kept for six years. HMRC can ask to see them within those periods.

Why should I reconcile my bank monthly?

It catches missing transactions, duplicates and coding errors while they are easy to trace, and it means your VAT return, payroll and year-end accounts are built on records that agree with the bank.
