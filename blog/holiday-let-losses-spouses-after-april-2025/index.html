Holiday let losses after 6 April 2025: what a husband and wife can do with what they carried forward

By James Fitzpatrick, AAT licensed · Accounting Solution · November 2026 · 4 min read

When the furnished holiday lettings rules ended on 6 April 2025, losses carried forward did not disappear. They became ordinary property business losses, which can be set against future UK property profits of the same person. For a jointly owned let, spouses are treated as sharing profits and losses equally unless they have told HMRC otherwise on Form 17, and each spouse uses their own share of the loss on their own return.

What changed on 6 April 2025

From 2025/26 a furnished holiday let is no longer a separate business. UK holiday lets are part of your UK property business, and overseas ones part of your overseas property business. HMRC's Property Income Manual (PIM4175) says losses made in 2024/25 or carried forward become losses of that ongoing property business. A UK loss can be set against UK property income and an overseas loss against overseas property income.

Other things that went with the regime:

  • mortgage interest is no longer a full deduction and is relieved as a basic rate tax credit, like other lettings
  • capital allowances on new furniture and equipment stop, with replacement of domestic items relief available for like-for-like replacements
  • Business Asset Disposal Relief on a sale is only available if the holiday letting business actually ended before 6 April 2025, within the usual time limits

A worked example for a couple

Tom and Jess own a holiday cottage equally. At 5 April 2025 the holiday let had a £6,000 loss to carry forward, so £3,000 each. From 2025/26 they let it as an ordinary property and make a £14,000 profit, which is £7,000 each. Each of them sets £3,000 of the old loss against their share and is taxed on £4,000. If Jess's loss were larger than her share of the cottage profit, the spare could only wait for her own future property profits. Tom cannot use it, and she cannot use his.

Who has which share

Profits and losses follow each owner's share of the property. Spouses and civil partners are treated as sharing equally by default, whatever the legal split of ownership. If the income really belongs to them in unequal shares, they can make a Form 17 declaration, which must reach HMRC within 60 days of the date it is signed. A declaration decides how future income is split from the date it is made. Do not assume it moves losses that have already been carried forward in each person's name.

If you use the property yourselves

The normal rule is that an expense must be incurred wholly and exclusively for the letting. Where a cost covers both lettings and your own stays, only the letting part counts. Keep a diary of the nights the family uses the property so the split is based on days, not guesswork. The old holiday let conditions on days available and days let no longer matter for tax, but the private use point does.

What to do on the 2025/26 return

  • Confirm the loss at 5 April 2025 for each spouse and keep the old working.
  • Report the cottage with the rest of the UK property pages, not as a separate FHL business.
  • Check each person's other UK property income, because it decides how fast the loss is used.
  • Look at the mortgage interest and equipment claims, which now follow the ordinary lettings rules.

Frequently Asked Questions

What happens to my furnished holiday let losses after 6 April 2025?

They are treated as losses of your ongoing UK property business, or overseas property business for overseas lets. They can be set against future property profits, including profits from a former holiday let and any other lettings you have.

Can my spouse use my brought-forward holiday let loss?

No. Each spouse is taxed separately, so each uses their own share of the loss against their own property income. A loss belonging to one spouse cannot be set against the other spouse's profits.

How are profits and losses split on a jointly owned holiday let?

Spouses and civil partners are treated as sharing profits and losses equally unless their entitlement to the income differs from their ownership shares and they have told HMRC through a Form 17 declaration, made within 60 days.

Can I still claim capital allowances on a holiday let?

Not for new furniture and equipment from 6 April 2025. Replacement of domestic items relief can apply to like-for-like replacements, and existing pool balances need reviewing with your accountant.

Do we have to apportion costs if we use the holiday let ourselves?

Yes. A cost is only allowable to the extent it was incurred wholly and exclusively for the letting. Where it covers both, split it, for example by nights used, and keep a record.
