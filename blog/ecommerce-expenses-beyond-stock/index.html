What expenses can I claim as an online seller besides stock?

By Accounting Solution · Last reviewed 29 September 2026 · 3 min read

Most costs of running the shop are allowable if they are wholly and exclusively for the business: platform and payment fees, postage and packaging, storage and fulfilment, software, advertising, photography, accountancy fees and a share of home costs. Personal spending, fines and client entertaining are not.

The usual deductions

CostNotes
Marketplace and payment feesAmazon, eBay, Etsy, Shopify, PayPal and Stripe fees are allowable, including those netted off your payout.
Postage, packaging and courier costsAllowable in full. Keep the platform's postage records.
Storage and fulfilmentFBA fees, third-party warehouse and rented storage.
Software and subscriptionsStore platforms, inventory and accounting software, design tools.
Advertising and sponsored listingsAllowable, including social media advertising and influencer fees.
Photography, props and listing designAllowable when for your product listings.
Accountancy and bank feesAllowable, including business account charges.
EquipmentPrinters, packing equipment, cameras and laptops, usually through the Annual Investment Allowance. Business cars are excluded from it.

Working from home and mileage

If you work from home you can claim a proportion of household bills, or use flat rates instead: £10 a month for 25 to 50 hours of business use, £18 for 51 to 100 hours, and £26 for 101 hours or more. Simplified mileage rates are available for business trips such as trips to the post office or a supplier, and you can claim either those or actual vehicle costs, not both.

Worked example. A seller spends about 60 hours a month working from home. On the flat rate that is £18 a month, or £216 for the year, without needing to apportion bills. If her actual extra costs were higher, she could claim those instead.

What you cannot claim

  • Personal spending and items bought for private use
  • Fines and penalties, including parking fines
  • Client entertainment
  • Depreciation as such (capital allowances are used instead)
  • Stock you have not yet sold, if you use traditional accounting (see stock accounting)

If your total business income is £1,000 or less, the trading allowance means you do not need to report it. Above that, you can deduct the £1,000 allowance instead of actual expenses, though it only helps if your real costs are lower. Frequently Asked Questions

What expenses can I claim as an online seller besides stock?

Most costs of running the shop are allowable if they are wholly and exclusively for the business: platform and payment fees, postage and packaging, storage and fulfilment, software, advertising, photography, accountancy fees and a share of home costs. Personal spending, fines and client entertaining are not.

Are Amazon and Etsy fees tax deductible?

Yes. Marketplace, referral and fulfilment fees are allowable business expenses. They are often netted off your payout, so use the platform's own sales report to see the full amount.

Can I claim for working from home as an online seller?

Yes, either a reasonable share of household costs or flat rates of £10, £18 or £26 a month depending on the hours you work at home. The flat rate is simpler and needs no bills.

Can I claim the trading allowance and expenses together?

No. You either deduct the £1,000 trading allowance in place of actual expenses, or you deduct your real expenses. Choose whichever gives the lower profit.
