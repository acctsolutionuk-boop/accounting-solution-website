How should I account for refunds and returns as an online seller?

By James Fitzpatrick, AAT licensed · Accounting Solution · Last reviewed 29 September 2026 · 3 min read

Record each refund as its own transaction linked to the original sale, not as a general expense. A refund reverses the sale and, if you are VAT-registered, the VAT you charged, and a returned item that goes back on the shelf also has to go back into your stock figure.

Why a lump-sum refunds line causes trouble

A refund is not an expense. It reduces your sales. If it is booked as a cost, turnover is overstated, which can distort your VAT threshold test and your VAT return. If it is left buried inside a payout, you cannot see your true refund rate.

Worked example. A customer buys a jacket for £120 including VAT (£100 plus £20 VAT). You refund £120. Sales fall by £100, output VAT falls by £20, and the jacket, which cost you £40, goes back into stock if it is resaleable. If it is damaged, the £40 becomes a stock loss instead.

VAT on refunds

If you are VAT-registered, you can reduce your output VAT when you refund a customer, but you need to keep a credit note (or equivalent record) showing the original sale and the refund. A partial refund reduces VAT in proportion. Marketplaces such as Amazon and Etsy generate their own refund records, so download them rather than relying on your bank statement.

If you refund after the VAT return has been filed, you adjust in the return for the period in which the credit note is issued, without having to amend the earlier return, provided the correction is within HMRC's limits.

Returns of stock and the platform payout

  • Add resaleable returns back to stock at their original cost. Write off damaged ones.
  • Reconcile each platform's settlement report to your books: gross sales, less refunds, less fees, equals the payout. If it does not match, something has been missed.
  • Watch return rates by product. A high rate can make an apparently profitable line lose money once postage, fees and write-offs are counted.
  • On platforms where the fee on a refunded sale is not returned to you, that fee is still a deductible cost.

Frequently Asked Questions

How should I account for refunds and returns as an online seller?

Record each refund as its own transaction linked to the original sale, not as a general expense. A refund reverses the sale and, if you are VAT-registered, the VAT you charged, and a returned item that goes back on the shelf also has to go back into your stock figure.

Do I get the VAT back when I refund a customer?

If you are VAT-registered, you can reduce the output VAT on your return when you refund, provided you keep a credit note or equivalent record showing the original sale and the amount refunded.

Are refunds an expense for tax?

No. A refund reduces your sales. Booking it as an expense overstates turnover, which affects your VAT threshold test, your VAT return and any profit-based figures.

What about returned stock that can be resold?

Put it back into stock at its original cost so that cost of goods sold is not overstated. If the item is damaged or cannot be resold, record it as a stock write-off instead.
