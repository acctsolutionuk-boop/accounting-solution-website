How do I keep clean books when selling across multiple platforms at once?

By James Fitzpatrick, AAT licensed · Accounting Solution · Last reviewed 29 September 2026 · 3 min read

Treat each platform's payout as a settlement to reconcile, not as your sales figure. Record gross sales, fees, refunds and postage separately for each channel, then check they add up to the amount that actually reached your bank. Recording bank deposits as sales gets turnover and profit wrong.

Why the bank deposit is the wrong number

Every platform pays you a net amount after taking fees, refunds and sometimes postage. If you book that payout as a sale, turnover is understated, fees are missed, and your VAT return and profit are wrong. That matters for the £90,000 VAT threshold test too, because it counts gross sales.

Worked example. An Etsy settlement pays £790 into the bank. The gross sales were £1,000. Etsy fees were £120, refunds £50 and postage labels £40.
LineAmount
Gross sales£1,000
Less platform fees−£120
Less refunds−£50
Less postage labels−£40
Payout to bank£790

In the books, sales are £1,000, and the fees, refunds and postage are recorded on their own lines. The bank shows £790, and the two agree.

A workable routine

  • Give each channel its own clearing (holding) account in your accounting software, so payouts can be matched.
  • Post each settlement report as one entry: gross sales, fees, refunds, postage, VAT if registered, and the net payout.
  • Reconcile the bank line to the clearing account. Any difference points to a missing fee, an adjustment or a reserve held by the platform.
  • Do it monthly. Unpicking a year of blended transactions is far harder than setting the routine up early.
  • Track VAT by channel, using the platform's VAT reports, if you are registered.

Tools such as A2X and Link My Books read settlement data from platforms including Amazon, Etsy, eBay and Shopify and post it into Xero or QuickBooks as one summary entry per settlement. They save time once volumes grow, though they still need someone to check the mapping. Related guides: refunds and returns and VAT across channels.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I keep clean books when selling across multiple platforms at once?

Treat each platform's payout as a settlement to reconcile, not as your sales figure. Record gross sales, fees, refunds and postage separately for each channel, then check they add up to the amount that actually reached your bank. Recording bank deposits as sales gets turnover and profit wrong.

Why is my bank balance different from my sales?

Platforms pay you net of fees, refunds and sometimes postage or reserves. The bank figure is the settlement, not the gross sales, so it will always be lower than your sales total.

Should I use A2X or Link My Books?

They are useful once you have several channels or high order volumes, because they post each settlement as one summary entry. A single low-volume shop can often be reconciled by hand with a spreadsheet.

How often should I reconcile marketplace payouts?

Monthly at minimum. Doing it as each settlement arrives is better, because differences such as missed fees or reserves are far easier to trace soon after they happen.
