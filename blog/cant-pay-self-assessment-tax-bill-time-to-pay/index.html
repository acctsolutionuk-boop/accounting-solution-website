Can't pay your Self Assessment bill by 31 January? How HMRC's Time to Pay works

By James Fitzpatrick, AAT licensed · Accounting Solution · November 2026 · 4 min read

If you cannot pay your Self Assessment bill in full by 31 January, you can usually set up a monthly payment plan with HMRC online. You must have filed your latest return, owe £30,000 or less, and be within 60 days of the payment deadline. Interest still runs, but a plan helps you avoid late payment penalties as long as you keep to it.

Who can set one up online

HMRC's online service is open to you if all of these apply:

  • you have filed your latest Self Assessment return
  • you owe £30,000 or less
  • you are within 60 days of the payment deadline
  • you have no other payment plans or debts with HMRC

HMRC cannot set up a plan until the return is filed, so a late return is the first thing to fix. For the 2025/26 bill due on 31 January 2027, the 60 days run out on 1 April 2027.

How long you get

The plan is paid in monthly instalments through a Direct Debit, and online plans normally run for up to 12 months. If you owe more than £30,000, need longer than 12 months, or are outside the 60 days, you can still ask for an arrangement but you have to contact HMRC directly, and it will want to know why you cannot pay and what you can afford.

What it costs

A plan is not free. Interest is charged from the original due date until the tax is paid, so a shorter plan costs less. The rate follows the Bank of England base rate and changes, so check the current figure on GOV.UK before you commit. Paying as much as you can up front reduces the interest.

Penalties

Late payment penalties on Self Assessment start at 5% of the unpaid tax once you are 30 days late, with further 5% charges at six and twelve months. HMRC says a payment plan helps you avoid these, provided you keep up the regular payments. The Low Incomes Tax Reform Group gives an example where the plan was in place by 2 March 2026, 30 days after the January deadline, so no late payment penalty arose. In practice, set the plan up before the 30-day point and do not leave it until day 60.

What the January bill usually contains

The 31 January payment is normally the balance of last year's tax plus the first payment on account towards the next year. The second payment on account is due on 31 July. A plan covers the amount that was due on 31 January, so the July payment still needs planning separately.

Keep your plan going

  • Make every Direct Debit on time. A missed payment can end the arrangement.
  • Pay the next bill when it falls due. A new debt can put the plan in doubt.
  • Use GOV.UK only. Scammers send messages offering to set up payment plans, so type the address yourself rather than following a link.

If the debt is a business tax

VAT, PAYE and corporation tax debts are not handled through the same online route, and arrangements are normally agreed with HMRC directly. If a company is behind on any of them, speak to us before the next deadline passes, because the way the first conversation goes sets the tone for the rest.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I set up an HMRC Time to Pay arrangement online?

Yes, if you have filed your latest Self Assessment return, owe £30,000 or less, are within 60 days of the payment deadline and have no other payment plans or debts with HMRC. You arrange it through GOV.UK.

How long can I take to pay?

Online plans are paid by monthly Direct Debit and normally run for up to 12 months. Longer arrangements need a conversation with HMRC.

Does a payment plan stop interest?

No. Interest is charged from the original due date until the tax is paid. The plan helps with penalties and debt collection, not with interest.

Will I still get a late payment penalty if I set up a plan?

HMRC says plans help you avoid late payment penalties as long as you make the regular payments. Setting one up before the 30-day point after the deadline avoids the first 5% penalty.

What if I owe more than £30,000?

You can still ask for an arrangement, but you cannot use the online service. You will need to contact HMRC directly and explain what you can pay.
