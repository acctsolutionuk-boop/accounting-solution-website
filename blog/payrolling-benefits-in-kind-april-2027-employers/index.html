Do I have to payroll company cars and private medical insurance from April 2027, and what happens to my P11D?

By James Fitzpatrick, AAT licensed · Accounting Solution · November 2026 · 4 min read

Yes, if you provide them. From 6 April 2027, HMRC requires employers to report company cars and car fuel, company vans and van fuel, and medical insurance through payroll, rather than on a P11D after the year ends. Most other benefits follow from 6 April 2028. Some of the detail is still in draft, so treat this as the position as GOV.UK stands today.

What changes, and when

HMRC's guidance for employers, published on 9 November 2026, says that from 6 April 2027 the tax and Class 1A National Insurance on company cars and fuel, company vans and fuel, and medical insurance must be collected through payroll software, reported on the Full Payment Submission. From 6 April 2028 all other benefits must be payrolled apart from loans and accommodation. HMRC says mandatory payrolling for loans and accommodation will be confirmed later.

Employers who want to payroll other benefits sooner can do so voluntarily, but they have to register with HMRC before the tax year starts.

What happens to the P11D

HMRC says payrolled benefits are not reported on a P11D or P11D(b). Valuation rules, exemptions and making-good rules stay as they are, and any underpayment at year end is picked up through the existing processes. Existing year-end reporting carries on for anything not yet brought into scope, so for a while you may run both: payroll for the benefits in scope and the P11D for the rest.

A Hove design studio has three staff. One has a company car, two are on a private medical policy the studio pays for, and one has a low-interest loan. From April 2027 the car and the medical cover go through payroll each month. The loan stays on the year-end route until HMRC confirms a date. The studio's P11D for 2027-28 is much shorter than before, but it does not disappear.

What it means for your employees

HMRC's advice is to tell staff that tax will be taken through payroll, that their payslip may look different, and that they will not pay more tax because of the change. Tax codes will not include adjustments for payrolled benefits, which means the cost comes through in the month rather than as a code change the following year. Employees get an annual statement showing what was payrolled.

Who is exempt

The guidance we read names one exemption: eligible globally mobile employees, who must apply for an exception by 5 April 2027. It does not set out a separate exemption for small employers. HMRC's policy paper also mentions a one-year easement from penalties for certain non-deliberate inaccuracies in the 2027-28 tax year, which is useful reassurance but not a reason to wait.

Still in draft

The policy paper, last updated on 28 October 2026, refers to draft PAYE regulations from July 2026, and says secondary legislation will settle which benefits are in scope and what the reporting must contain. Things could still move. We have not quoted any rates or valuation figures here because they depend on the benefit and the year.

What to do now

  • List every benefit you currently report on a P11D, and who receives it.
  • Ask your payroll software provider whether it supports the Full Payment Submission reporting for benefits, and from which version.
  • Decide whether to register early for voluntary payrolling of other benefits.
  • Tell the employees affected before the first payslip changes.

Sources: GOV.UK: prepare for mandatory payrolling of benefits in kind · HMRC policy paper: changes to reporting of benefits in kind from April 2027 · Mandatory reporting of benefits in kind in RTI from April 2027. General guidance at 25 November 2026; your own facts decide the answer.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which benefits must be payrolled from April 2027?

From 6 April 2027 employers must report company cars and fuel, company vans and fuel, and medical insurance through payroll. GOV.UK says most other benefits, except loans and accommodation, must follow from 6 April 2028.

Do payrolled benefits still go on a P11D?

No. GOV.UK says payrolled benefits are not reported on a P11D or P11D(b). Income Tax and Class 1A National Insurance are dealt with through payroll software in real time instead.

What about employer loans and accommodation?

They are outside the 2027 and 2028 start dates. HMRC says mandatory payrolling for them will be confirmed at a later date, so the existing year-end reporting continues for those until it does.

Is there an exemption for small employers?

The GOV.UK guidance we read does not set out a small-employer exemption. The only exemption it names is for eligible globally mobile employees, who have to apply for an exception by 5 April 2027.

Will my employees pay more tax because of this?

HMRC tells employers to explain that employees will not pay more tax because of the change. Tax codes will not include adjustments for payrolled benefits, and employees get an annual payrolled benefits statement.

Is the detail final yet?

Not entirely. HMRC's policy paper says the draft PAYE regulations are dated July 2026 and that secondary legislation will set the final scope, so check the GOV.UK pages before you finalise your payroll set-up.
