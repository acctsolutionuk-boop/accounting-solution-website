VAT calculator UK: add or remove VAT, and check the registration threshold

The sums behind it

  • Net to gross: multiply by 1.20 at the standard rate, 1.05 at the reduced rate. The VAT is the difference.
  • Gross to net: divide by 1.20 or 1.05. Do not take 20% off the gross, because VAT is charged on the net price.
  • Zero rate: no VAT is added, but the sale counts towards your threshold and you can still reclaim VAT on related costs once registered.

Registering for VAT

  • Register when taxable turnover over any rolling 12 months goes above £90,000, within 30 days of the end of the month you passed it.
  • Register by the end of a 30-day period if you expect that period alone to bring in more than £90,000.
  • You can register voluntarily below the threshold. That can suit a business with large VAT-bearing costs, or one that sells mainly to other VAT-registered businesses.
  • Deregistration is possible once you expect taxable turnover to be £88,000 or less over the next 12 months.

Selling online

Marketplace and own-website sales both count towards the threshold. VAT on EU sales, imports and digital products has its own rules, which we cover in the guides below.

Frequently asked questions

What is the VAT registration threshold for 2026/27?

You must register for VAT when your taxable turnover over any rolling 12 months goes above £90,000, or when you expect to pass £90,000 in the next 30 days alone. The test runs on any 12-month period, not the tax year, so check it every month.

How do I add 20% VAT to a price?

Multiply the net price by 1.20. For example £1,000 plus 20% VAT is £1,200, of which £200 is VAT. At the 5% reduced rate you multiply by 1.05, and at the zero rate the price does not change.

How do I take VAT out of a price that already includes it?

Divide the gross price by 1.20 at the standard rate. £1,200 including VAT is £1,000 net with £200 of VAT. Taking 20% off £1,200 gives £960, which is wrong, because VAT is charged on the net price and not the gross.

What are the UK VAT rates?

There are three: the standard rate of 20% on most goods and services, a reduced rate of 5% on things like domestic fuel and power, and a zero rate on things like most food, books and children's clothes. Some supplies are exempt, which is different from zero-rated, and exempt sales do not count towards the registration threshold.

Do online sellers count sales from Amazon, eBay and Shopify towards the threshold?

Yes. Sales you make as a UK-established seller through a marketplace or your own store count towards the £90,000 taxable turnover test, along with every other taxable sale you make. Add them up across all channels before you check.

When can I deregister for VAT?

You can apply to deregister if you expect your taxable turnover over the next 12 months to be £88,000 or less. Check the effect first: you stop reclaiming VAT on your costs, and you may have to account for VAT on stock and assets you still hold.
